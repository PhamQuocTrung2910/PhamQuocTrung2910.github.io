The hot take

Entry-level shouldn’t mean “3 years’ experience required.”

I had it easier than most. I didn’t have to work much part-time during uni, so I had the time to chase an internship, and I landed one. It still wasn’t enough to tick the experience box on most graduate listings.

If that’s the easy version, think about everyone who was busy staying afloat, working shifts to cover living expenses, with internships in Australia often unpaid. A graduate role that asks for a work history ends up measuring who had the time and money to get one. Hire for how someone learns. I’ll show you what that looks like below.

The story so far

Chapter 1 · Semester 1, 2024

Polar Night

A survival horror prototype. Four of us, astronauts crash-landed on an alien planet, and a Wendigo-like creature stalking them. You switch between two characters, and the engine’s light is the only thing keeping the creature away. Run out of power and you’re on the menu.

I designed the Game Menus, and sourced the sound for the game, We as a team tested, broke it, and tested again.

Side quest · Software Development & Evolution Semester 2 2024

The teammate who went quiet

Team of four, one person not pulling their weight. I was properly frustrated. Instead of complaining about it, I talked to the rest of the team, we redistributed the work, and we got on with it.

Be annoyed for a minute, then get back to work.

Chapter 2 · August to October 2025

Focus Bear

Three months as a Mobile Development Intern on a live React Native app with 10,000+ users. I added PostHog event tracking across key user flows, reproduced and fixed reported bugs using Ngrok, and joined daily standups with a team of seven.

The real lesson wasn’t code. It was communication, meetings, deadlines and planning, the things that actually make a team ship features fast.

The part that hurt

I struggled to finish a single issue while my peers excelled. I thought I was pretty good at coding and problem-solving, so that was a massive hit to my ego. It was humbling, and it’s where I learned the most.

Chapter 3 · Semester 2, 2025 to Semester 1, 2026

LIDA, an app for a composting machine

A real client with a real composting machine, and a Flutter app built to pair with it and track what it’s doing. Me and my team gathered requirements straight from the client, turned them into features, and adjusted as his feedback came in.

I built a voice assistant for hands-free navigation across 6 screens, with 8 voice commands for setup, plus an on-demand interactive tutorial that walks first-time users through every core section. I also contributed to writing the sprint reports for our six-person team so the next semester’s group could pick it up cleanly.

Chapter 4 · The last unit

Data Structures and Patterns

The final hurdle before the degree: a written code exam. Written exams are where I struggle most, so I spent the whole semester learning every corner of the language it was based on. Study sessions with friends, a lot of Vietnamese coffee, and late nights in the library.

I passed :).
